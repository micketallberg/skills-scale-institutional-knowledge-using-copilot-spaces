# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This folder collects OctoAcme’s project-management guidance for cross-functional work. Projects move through five lifecycle stages: initiation, planning, execution, release, and close/retrospective. The process aims to deliver customer value in small, testable increments, with clear ownership, evidence-informed decisions, and space for team feedback and learning.

At initiation, teams clarify the problem, intended outcomes, success measures, stakeholders, risks, and rough timeline before deciding whether to proceed. Planning turns approved work into a prioritized, estimated backlog with acceptance criteria and a Definition of Done, while establishing milestones, dependencies, responsibilities, and an initial QA approach. During execution, work is tracked on a project board, progress and blockers are reviewed in team standups and regular delivery/PM syncs, and demos or reviews provide opportunities to inspect progress and adapt.

The Project Manager coordinates delivery, schedules, risks, dependencies, and communications; the Product Manager/Product Lead owns product outcomes and prioritization; developers build and test; QA validates quality and acceptance; and stakeholders provide input and approvals. Teams keep status and decisions visible through shared project documentation, regular updates, risk registers, and escalation paths. Quality practices include clear acceptance criteria, code review, automated tests and linting, security scans, and appropriate integration, smoke, and manual acceptance testing. Releases are verified before and after deployment, with rollback or mitigation plans prepared. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, milestones, or incidents convert lessons into owned, trackable improvements.
